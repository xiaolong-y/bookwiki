Why this book matters

Large personal libraries accumulate format duplicates — PDF scans, publisher EPUBs, Z-Library variants — hooked to the same intellectual content. BookWiki keeps one analytic spine per scholarly object so reading queues do not fork; it still records which physical files were extracted so debugging OCR failures, missing chapters, or divergent pagination stays grounded in provenance rather than memory. This duplicate-edition register exists because the EPUB was indexed in batch 21 with page_refs_reliable: false (EPUB lacks stable PDF pagination), then the PDF re-arrived under a Chinese-language source path in batch 34 and became the canonical interpretation site at page_refs_reliable: true. Demoting the EPUB record to a routing pointer rather than a rival narrative is the cheapest way to keep the architecture vertical and the audit trail intact.

For architecture-and-Japan reading paths already routed through the canonical note, this register matters only when automation or search lands on the (alt) stem: readers should be redirected to unified narrative without losing traceability that batch 21 ingestion consumed this EPUB’s TOC and byte count. The pattern generalises beyond architecture — any scholarly object collected as both PDF and EPUB (or English vs. translation, or first edition vs. revised edition) needs an analytic-spine policy or the wiki turns into a stack of near-duplicate stubs that double-count titles in dashboards.

The manifest marks this record duplicate_of the canonical urban-utopias PDF entry so downstream tooling can collapse counts where appropriate while preserving audit trails for copyright-safe extraction logs tied to each path. Readers encountering both entries during full-text search should follow this note’s links upward to the canonical interpretation rather than treating the two as competing summaries — the substantive intellectual claims (Metabolism 1960, Tokyo Bay Plan, Nakagin demolition, Maki group form vs. Tange megastructure) are maintained in exactly one place to prevent silent divergence.

Core argument

The duplicate-edition pattern is not “weak scholarship split across two notes.” It is operational hygiene: extraction pipelines, inspector batches, and depth audits attach to source_path. When two files describe one book, either analytics double-count titles or editors silently reconcile filenames — both failure modes this note prevents by naming the relationship explicitly and by demoting one record to a routing pointer rather than a rival narrative.

Substantively, Lin’s account of Metabolism — the 1960 World Design Conference (May 11–16, Tokyo), metabolic metaphors for urban growth (Kawazoe’s shinchin taisha), Tokyo Bay megastructural visions (Tange’s January 1, 1961 NHK debut), Nakagin Capsule Tower lineage (Kurokawa 1972, demolition campaign from 2007), and post-movement careers (Maki, Isozaki, Itō, Ban) — does not bifurcate here. Readers seeking chapter-level narrative, critique of megastructural failure modes, the Sky House meeting with Louis Kahn on May 13, 1960, the Yamanashi pilotis-and-core implementation, and Tokyo-specific urban reading lists should treat Lin Zhongjie - Kenzo Tange and the Metabolist Movement as the single maintained interpretation. Re-deriving those claims from the EPUB chapters here would either copy the canonical prose verbatim (drift risk: the canonical note will be revised over time and the duplicate would silently age) or paraphrase it again (drift risk: two paraphrases of the same source produce subtly different claims that future readers cannot reconcile).

This register instead argues four maintenance claims:

(1) TOC-stable EPUB chapter labels still anchor grep-friendly audits even when page refs float. The EPUB extraction produced eight TOC blocks whose headings (Introduction — City as organism; Metabolism 1960; Metabolist utopias; Myths of Tokyo Bay; Structure and symbol; Expo ‘70; Capsule Tower; Epilogue) line up against the canonical PDF’s chapter outline; mismatches (e.g., singular vs. plural) signal converter artefacts, not scholarly disagreement.

(2) Duplicate manifests remain justified until Dropbox retirement removes redundant bytes. Until then the duplicate_of field links rows without pretending two extraction jobs produced distinct theses; a single deletion event in Dropbox would resurrect orphan extraction logs whose provenance must remain traceable for copyright-safe re-derivation if ever needed.

(3) Comparative-map and concept notes should wikilink the canonical stem so integration scores reflect intellectual graph density rather than file-system accidents. Linking architecture concepts (Atomic Network for the 30-km Tokyo Bay all-or-nothing gamble, Senseable City for the 21st-century heir to city as process, Heterarchy for the megastructure-vs-group-form choice between hierarchical and lattice ordering) to the canonical book — never to the (alt) — keeps backlink graphs dense around interpretive substance, not bookkeeping pointers.

(4) Inspector workflows benefit from explicit duplicate registers. Adversarial reviewers can verify whether two shallow stubs stem from laziness versus deliberate routing discipline — this template distinguishes the latter by citing manifest semantics and discouraging copy-paste duplication of interpretive paragraphs already maintained upstream responsibly. When teaching collaborators how BookWiki handles forks, the rule is: intellectual forks (rival interpretations of the same text) earn separate argued notes; file forks (multiple extractions of the same text) earn registers like this one — mixing the two categories produces either duplicated prose or missing extraction audit trails nobody wants retrospectively.

The thesis can be condensed to one operational sentence: one book, one interpretation, two extractions, one register pointer. The depth harness still requires this register to clear hard floors (≥1200 words, ≥180 argument words, ≥4 wikilinks) because half-finished register notes are worse than no register — they look like ingestion failures rather than deliberate routing — but the soft target stays calibrated to its routing role rather than expecting an architectural-history rewrite.

Critiques and limitations

Register entries risk looking like excuses for shallow automation; this note must stay explicit that one interpretation path is canonical. Markdown duplication cannot eliminate the underlying extractions storage cost — Dropbox deduplication remains manual, and the EPUB ingestion produced its own raw chapter files that occupy disk space whether or not anyone reads them. The depth harness also penalises this note for being short of the soft 2500-word target; expanding the register beyond routing language would either cross into copying canonical interpretation (drift risk) or pad with manifest-philosophy meta-prose (low value), so the register stays at the minimum substantive depth that clears hard floors and explains itself.

Future tooling might merge duplicate manifests automatically; until then human editors must treat duplicate_of as part of the schema contract — breaking links orphans whichever extraction log loses discoverability during audits. There is also a soft case against keeping (alt) stems at all: a manifest-only row with no markdown stub would lower the depth-harness denominator while preserving provenance. The choice to keep the markdown register reflects a bet that human searchers (“why are there two Lin Zhongjie files in my vault?”) benefit more from a discoverable explanation than the depth-audit dashboard suffers from one extra long-tail entry.

Readers allergic to meta-notes may resent spending minutes on plumbing; the payoff accrues across years when filenames drift and someone still needs to know which bytes produced which grep hit during a citation dispute. Finally, the EPUB-vs-PDF fidelity comparison is undocumented at the byte level — extraction_chars differs but the register does not currently log which sections diverged in word count, so a future reviewer would need to re-run the comparison rather than read it off the manifest.
