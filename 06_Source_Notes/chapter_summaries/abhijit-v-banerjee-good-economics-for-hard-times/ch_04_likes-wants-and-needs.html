Likes, Wants, and Needs

Likes, Wants, and Needs

Ch.4 — Likes, Wants, and Needs

Headline. Becker-Stigler’s “De Gustibus Non Est Disputandum” doctrine — that preferences are stable, coherent, and not to be questioned by economists — is wrong about racism, peer effects, identity, and most of what drives political polarization. Preferences are radically context-dependent. And yet this is liberating, because it means preferences can be changed by altering context — without coercion or propaganda.

The Becker-Stigler hope refuted. Bigoted bakers should go bankrupt because of lost business. They don’t, because (a) like-minded customers reward them, and (b) preferences are themselves shaped by community membership. Bigotry can be good business and good politics.

Standard preferences can rationalize fads (Banerjee 1992, herd behavior). People may rationally join the crowd on the train platform if they suspect others have private information. One artificial up-vote on a comment website (random-assignment RCT) raises top-grade probability significantly months later — the cascade compounds. So fads do not refute standard preferences.

Norms can be sustained without true preferences (folk theorem). Caste discrimination, racial discrimination, and small-community cooperation (Ostrom Swiss-cheese alpine commons, Kenya/Sri Lanka fisheries) all work via the same logic: norm-violators excluded, exclusion-violators excluded, etc. Pretty much any norm — including bad ones — can be self-enforcing. The film Samskara iconic case: Brahmin who slept with a low-caste prostitute; no one will cremate him for fear of pollution; body rots in public. Caste-panchayat enforcing tradition over law (the 14-yr-old rape victim beaten for going to police).

Statistical discrimination can produce racism without preference. “Ban-the-box” study: when employers can’t see felony status on application, they statistically discriminate against black applicants (assuming higher prior conviction rate). Pre-BTB white-vs-black callback gap was 7%; post-BTB it became 43%. The well-intentioned reform made things worse. Trust-game experiment with Ashkenazi senders sending less to Eastern Jewish receivers — but Eastern Jews returned money at exactly the same rate as Ashkenazis. The stereotype was unfair, and even Eastern senders had absorbed it.

Self-fulfilling prophecies & stereotype threat. Steele’s classic experiment: black students score lower when test framed as IQ test; white students score worse when told the experiment is about Asian math superiority. Princeton golf experiment: same students; race primed → black score worse on “general sports performance”; “natural athletic ability” framing → black score better; “sports intelligence” framing → white score better. Everyone has bought the stereotypes. In a French grocery, biased supervisors managed minority cashiers less well (didn’t visit cashier stations); minority productivity suffered, “confirming” the prejudice. Bangladesh women managers: technically as good as men but workers didn’t listen → lines underperformed → confirming bias.

Acting white norm. L.A. Hispanic high schoolers offered free SAT prep: take-up was 11pp lower when the choice would be made public — for non-honors students. They feared “acting white.” Asian students don’t have this norm. History selects which norms get sustained.

Coherent arbitrariness (Ariely-Loewenstein-Prelec, Kahneman-Thaler endowment). Mug/pen endowment: WTA 2-3× WTP. Trackball/wine bid manipulated by writing-down-of-SSN-last-two-digits as anchor: SSN >80 bid 200-350% more than SSN <20. People don’t have absolute valuations. Robbers Cave (Sherif): randomly divided 11-12-yr-olds → animosity emerged → joint task → animosity dissolved. Rwanda Belgian-fabricated Hutu-Tutsi distinction → 1994 genocide. Preferences are partly invented and partly malleable.

Self-reinforcing segregation. Schelling tipping points (Card: most US cities tip at 5–15% black share). Homophily means even mild own-group preference + crowded out-group → near-total separation. US cross-racial marriage 50 years after Loving v. Virginia: 1 in 6 newlyweds. India 74% endogamy. Mostly because finding similar-to-self is easier within-group.

Echo chambers. Twitter politically-engaged users: 84% of conservative followers are conservative; 69% of liberal followers are liberal. 92% of liberal messages from liberal sources, 93% of conservative from conservative. Even on non-political topics. Gentzkow-Shapiro: party-affiliation prediction from a single sentence of congressional speech rose from 55% (1873-early-1990s) to 83% (2007-09). Newt Gingrich’s 1994 takeover and the rise of political consultants is a turning point.

Are echo chambers from the internet? Mixed evidence. Gentzkow-Shapiro 2009 isolation index for online news (7.5pp) is lower than offline news. Polarization is rising even among 65+ (least online) and rose less among 18-39 (most online). Cable TV (Fox shifted right since 2004) plays a key role. Korean online experiment: users who chose their news sources moderated more than users force-fed random sources — because they understood and discounted bias. The internet’s filter-bubble automatic customization is the real problem.

Motivated beliefs (Bénabou-Tirole). We don’t reason from neutral premises. We protect a self-image (intelligent, kind, etc.). We rationalize prejudice in the language of “objective fact.” Calling people “racist” or “deplorable” assaults that self-image and entrenches the position. Calling racists “fine people” lets them feel better about themselves and doubles down (Trump strategy, “morally reprehensible but effective”).

Wilson on inner-city ghettos applies to white Rust Belt. When the social problems described for black inner cities in 1997 (joblessness, family dissolution, social disorder) describe Vance’s white Appalachia in 2017, the white community’s self-image-defense response is to ratchet up the caricature of the other — single-mother-on-welfare becomes single-mother-gang-member.

Operationally tractable interventions.

  • Contact hypothesis (Allport 1954, 27-RCT review): contact reduces prejudice if status is equal, goals shared, cooperation real, authorities support it. Random college roommate with minority-race student: white more likely to support affirmative action and continue mixed friendships in year 2.
  • Delhi private-school RP (rich+poor reservation policy): kids who ran the relay-race-with-playdate exercise selected better runners regardless of caste only if exposed to mixed schools. Familiarity dissolved the playdate-aversion.
  • UP cricket league RCT: mixed-caste teams produced more cross-caste friendships AFTER. But teams that competed against other-caste opponents made less progress. Competition undermines contact. France 1998/2018 World Cup soccer was the cooperative-shared-purpose case.
  • Schelling-aware housing: Singapore’s per-block ethnic quotas; Paris housing projects integrated into fancy neighborhoods. Boston busing was unpopular but METCO program (busing minority kids to majority schools) worked, no harm to majority kids.

The four operational implications. (1) Calling people racists deepens racism. (2) Voters don’t have absolute racial preferences — Mayawati 2007 UP shift to broad-poor-coalition; deep-red Medicaid-expansion ballot wins in Utah/Nebraska/Idaho. (3) Bigoted voting is often indifference to the political system: simple street-theater messaging (“don’t vote on caste”) moved 10% of UP voters away from caste-party preferences. (4) Best route to combatting prejudice is to give citizens a reason to engage with policy substance — restore public conversation credibility.

Bridge to next chapters. Subsequent chapters examine substance — growth, inequality, climate, automation, government — that an engaged citizenry would need to reason about. The endorsement here is that public conversation can be restored if the substance is delivered with humility and structural respect for ordinary people’s dignity.
