Sebastian Mallaby The Infinity Machine — Chapter Deep Dives

Sebastian Mallaby The Infinity Machine — Chapter Deep Dives

Sebastian Mallaby The Infinity Machine — Chapter Deep Dives

15 chapters

Ch.1 — Introduction — The Sweetness

Mallaby frames the book around two parallel objects: Demis Hassabis as a person, and DeepMind as the institution he founded to chase machine superintelligence.

Ch.2 — Destiny

Hassabis’s biography is laid out as the input to a recurring trait bundle: variance-seeking, conflict-tolerance, civilization-scale framing.

Ch.3 — “Deep Philosophical Questions”

Hassabis enters Cambridge in 1994, refusing Molyneux’s £500,000 retention offer (≈$1.7M today).

Ch.4 — The Jedi (Elixir, 1998-2005)

Hassabis founds Elixir in July 1998 with David Silver, Joe McDonagh, and Tim Clarke.

Ch.5 — The Gang of Three

The chapter assembles the trio that founds DeepMind: Demis Hassabis, Shane Legg, Mustafa Suleyman.

Ch.6 — Founding DeepMind

August 14, 2010, Singularity Summit, San Francisco. Hassabis, Legg, and Suleyman come desperate for capital.

Ch.7 — Atari (DQN, 2012-2013)

October 2012, Florence: Alex Krizhevsky (Hinton’s protégé) presents the deep-learning ImageNet result at ECCV — his network is roughly twice as accurate as the runner-up; Hinton, Krizhevsky and Sutskever sell their three-person company to…

Ch.8 — 8 — Thiel Trouble & Get Google (2012-2014)

October 8, 2012: Luke Nosek flies back from a SpaceX Falcon 9 launch on Musk’s private jet, alongside Larry Page.

Ch.9 — Intuition (AlphaGo, 2014-2016)

May 2014, Mountain View. Hassabis tells Sergey Brin that DeepMind’s Atari techniques can solve Go.

Ch.10 — Out of Eden (Safety Board Failure & OpenAI’s Birth, 2015)

The chapter dissects the August 14, 2015 Ethics & Safety Board meeting at SpaceX and the subsequent founding of OpenAI as a singleton thesis vs. multiparty thesis schism among AI’s lead actors.

Ch.11 — 14 — P0 Plus Plus, NHS, and the Project Mario Failure (2015-2019)

The chapters trace Mustafa Suleyman’s parallel AI-for-good experiment with the British NHS and his attempt with Hassabis to spin DeepMind out from Google as Project Mario.

Ch.12 — 13 — The Agent and the Transformer (2017-2019)

The chapter is the book’s central technical-strategic crossroads: the moment when DeepMind’s reinforcement-learning bet becomes contested by OpenAI’s transformer-based language-modeling bet.

Ch.13 — Fermat for Biology (AlphaFold, 2016-2020)

The protein-folding chapter is the book’s clearest demonstration of Hassabis’s pivot doctrine and the eventual Nobel Prize.

RaceGPT (2020-2023)

The chapters track the rise of OpenAI from “noise-making” lab to industry-defining force, the ChatGPT shock of November 2022, and DeepMind’s belated transformer pivot.

Ch.15 — 20 + Epilogue — Step by Step, Comeback, and Turing’s Champion (2024-2026)

The closing chapters trace Google DeepMind’s comeback through reasoning models and agentic AI, the rise of Chinese competition, and Hassabis’s October 2024 Nobel Prize.
