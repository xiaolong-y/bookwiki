Ch.1 — Front matter (copyright, contents, acclaim)

Three pages of EPUB front matter (copyright, table of contents, acclaim quotes).

Ch.2 — Prologue — A Good Man in a Storm

Kidder opens not in a basement lab but on a 35-foot sloop in a north-easterly gale en route from Portland, Maine, to New York.

Ch.3 — 1 — How to Make a Lot of Money

Data General Corporation, headquartered in Westborough, Massachusetts (Building 14A/B, “Webo”), is by 1979 the third-largest minicomputer maker after DEC, with $507.5M revenue and ~70,700 cumulative computers shipped since founding in 1968…

Ch.4 — 2 — The Wars

The chapter introduces Tom West as Eclipse Group leader and traces the internal politics that produced the “Eagle” project.

Ch.5 — 3 — Building a Team

The chapter is a tour of the Westborough basement and an account of how the Eagle team was hired.

Ch.6 — 4 — Wallach’s Golden Moment

A character study of Steve Wallach, the Eagle architect, and a chapter on the architectural design itself.

Ch.7 — 5 — Midnight Programmer

A character study of Carl Alsing (microcode lieutenant) and a layered explanation of how a computer actually computes — high-level language → assembly → microcode → gates and electrons.

Ch.8 — 6 — Flying Upside Down

The chapter formalizes “the rules” of the Eagle game and walks through the design phase (summer 1978 to early 1979).

Ch.9 — 7 — La Machine

Debugging begins (early 1979) with two prototype Eagles named Coke and Gollum (originally Coke and Pepsi; Holberger demanded Gollum after Tolkien).

Ch.10 — 8 — The Wonderful Micromachines

A study of the Microteam at work, organized around two figures (sublieutenant Chuck Holland and prodigy programmer Neal Firth) and two tools (UINST and the simulator).

Ch.11 — 9 — A Workshop

A character study of West outside the office, organized around his 1780 farmhouse and basement workshop.

Ch.12 — 10 — The Case of the Missing NAND Gate

The longest and most procedurally detailed chapter — a single multi-day debugging case that crystallizes the team’s craft.

Ch.13 — 11 — Shorter Than a Season

A short chapter on burnout, illustrated through Hardy Boy Josh Rosen — one of the team’s most credentialed engineers (designed the ALU board; “I was the star at Special Systems,” Rosen says of his prior post inside Data General).

Ch.14 — 12 — Pinball

The chapter introduces the book’s most-quoted concept — pinball — and lays out the team’s complaints about resources, recognition, and West’s distance.

Ch.15 — 13 — Going to the Fair

The team rides a chartered bus to the National Computer Conference (NCC) in New York City — an interlude that puts the Eagle project in a wider industry context.

Ch.16 — 14 — The Last Crunch

The chapter covers August through early October 1979 — the final crunch through the slowdown, the false starts on Adventure, and Eagle’s transition to a working computer.

Ch.17 — 15 — Canards

The chapter is the book’s interpretive coda — the question of who actually invented Eagle, framed against the labor-and-craft argument that closes the book.

Ch.18 — 16 — Dinosaurs

The closing chapter is about West’s exit from the Eclipse Group and the team’s dispersal.

Ch.19 — Epilogue

Before the team formally disbanded, the Eclipse Group met to determine which engineers had contributed to Eagle’s patentable features.
