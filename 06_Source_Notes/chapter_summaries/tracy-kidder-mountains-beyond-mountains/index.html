Tracy Kidder Mountains Beyond Mountains — Chapter Deep Dives

27 chapters

Ch.3 — How they met (Mirebalais, December 1994)

Kidder, embedded with U.S. Army Special Forces during the post-coup intervention, meets Paul Farmer outside a small army outpost in Mirebalais.

Ch.4 — The Brigham (December 1999)

Kidder spends a day shadowing Farmer at Brigham and Women’s Hospital, four floors down in radiology.

Ch.5 — Zanmi Lasante (January 2000)

The 35-mile drive from Port-au-Prince takes three hours up the wreck-strewn National Highway 3. At the top of a steep incline, headlights pick out the wall and gate of Zanmi Lasante (“Partners In Health” in Creole) — a walled medical citad…

Ch.6 — The hike to Morne Michel (the noncompliance study)

Kidder follows Farmer on a five-hour round-trip hike to Morne Michel, the most distant village in Zanmi Lasante’s catchment, to find one TB patient who missed his monthly appointment.

Ch.7 — The Blue Bird Inn and the Lady Gin (Farmer’s childhood)

Born 1959 in North Adams, Massachusetts. Father — Paul Sr., “the Warden” / “Elbows” — moved family to Birmingham, Alabama (1966) seeking sales work, then to Brooksville, Florida (1971).

Ch.8 — Duke, Virchow, and arrival in Haiti (1983)

Photographic memory for friends; legions of female friends; food fights and Sound of Music sing-alongs while studying.

Ch.9 — Meeting Ophelia (Mirebalais, 1983)

Ophelia Dahl, daughter of writer Roald Dahl and actress Patricia Neal, arrives in Haiti at 18 as a volunteer for Eye Care Haiti.

Ch.10 — Cange and Léogâne (1983-1984)

the squatter settlement of “water refugees” displaced by the Péligre Dam, half a mile up the road from the reservoir.

Ch.11 — Building Zanmi Lasante; meeting Tom White (1985-1986)

The Harvard-Haiti commute begins to forge a religious orientation.

Ch.12 — Founding PIH; meeting Jim Kim; the broken leg (1986-1988)

the muzzle falls off (baboukèt la tonbe) after Baby Doc flees.

Ch.13 — Aristide, the 1990 election, the 1991 coup

Haitian army rule 1986-1990, with U.S. assistance Farmer documents through declassified material.

Ch.14 — The junta years; The Uses of Haiti (1991-1994)

After the Cédras coup, Farmer continues edgy transit between Cange and Boston.

Ch.15 — The great epi divide and MDR-TB

Short conceptual chapter introducing the “great epi divide” — Farmer’s own coinage — for the partition of humanity between populations who die in their seventies of aging-body diseases and populations who die ten to forty years earlier of…

Ch.16 — Carabayllo and Father Jack (1994-1995)

Father Jack Roussin — beefy Boston priest, “a character,” former pastor of St.

Ch.17 — The ten patients (1996)

Kidder’s first arrival at Carabayllo at night. Lima’s northern outskirts spread up steep gray-brown hills with no soil, lit by absurdly oversized highway-style pylons.

Ch.18 — Médicos aventureros (1996-1997)

Hard counsel from Michael Iseman (world’s foremost MDR-TB clinical authority, National Jewish in Denver): even in 1993 his cure rate was ~60%, costs running as high as $250,000 per case, two years of weak side-effect-laden second-line drug…

Ch.19 — Hepatitis A; Christian’s recovery; the doctor’s daughter

Farmer marries Didi Bertrand in Cange in 1996. About 4,000 attend; Jim Kim and a Duke friend best men; Père Lafontant and three priests preside.

Ch.20 — DOTS-plus and the Goldfarb confrontation (April 1998)

Howard Hiatt convenes a special meeting at the American Academy of Arts and Sciences in Boston to present early Carabayllo results: of the first 53 patients treated, >85% cured.

Ch.21 — Driving down the price (1998-2000)

Cost-effectiveness analysis has become the gatekeeping discipline of international health: imported from engineering, war, and medicine; deployed by big donors as a test you have to pass.

Ch.22 — A light month for travel (early 2000)

“Paul and Jim mobilized the world to accept drug-resistant TB as a soluble problem.” He urges Farmer to step out of one-on-one Cange clinical work and become a global executive on TB, AIDS, and malaria — but Farmer refuses.

Ch.23 — Cuba (early 2000)

“Look! Only ninety miles from Haiti and look! Trees! Crops!” He admires Cuban medicine on health-statistics grounds, not ideological grounds.

Ch.24 — Paris (Catherine’s second birthday)

Kidder is sleeping-pill-fogged. Farmer’s wife Didi has been studying the records French slavers kept of their West African commerce — the city’s beauty has accordingly soured for both of them.

Ch.25 — Moscow and the Russian prisons

Russia is now the largest TB project Farmer is engaged with.

Ch.26 — The Gates grant; “If Paul is the model, we’re golden”

the Gates Foundation grants PIH and a coalition of partners $45 million to wipe out MDR-TB in Peru — virtually everything Jim asked for.

Ch.27 — The boy John

The most narrative-dense and emotionally costly chapter in the book.

Ch.28 — The hike to Casse and “the long defeat”

December (~2 months after John’s death). Kidder returns to Cange.

Ch.29 — Afterword (June 2002 update)

WHO adopts new MDR-TB guidelines virtually identical to PIH’s Carabayllo protocols.
