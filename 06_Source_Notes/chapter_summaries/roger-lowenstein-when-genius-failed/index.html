Ch.3 — Introduction — The Fed Summit, September 23, 1998

New York Fed president William McDonough convenes the heads of every major Wall Street bank — Bankers Trust, Bear Stearns, Chase, Goldman, J.P.

Ch.4 — Meriwether (Salomon Arbitrage Group, 1977-1991)

The founding revelation (1979). A securities dealer J.F. Eckstein faces ruin on a Treasury-bill-futures-vs-bills convergence trade that has gone against him.

Ch.5 — Hedge Fund (1993-Feb 1994 fundraising and LTCM launch)

~3,000 hedge funds, 300Bvs3.2T in mutual funds. Soros’s 1992 sterling break ($1B profit) had glamorized the form; investors wanted “bold AND safe — not the riskiest investing style but the most certain.” LTCM was designed for that gap.

Ch.6 — On the Run (1994-1995, first two years)

The lucky entry timing (1994). Greenspan’s Feb-1994 quarter-point rate hike triggered a bond rout.

Ch.7 — Dear Investors (the Merton-Scholes risk philosophy)

Meriwether’s investor letter — drafted with Merton-Scholes — calculated odds of loss to “precise mathematical degrees”: 12% chance of losing ≥5% in any year, with explicit probabilities for 10/15/20% losses.

Ch.8 — Tug-of-War (1996, peak profit and the warrant obsession)

By spring 1996: $140B assets, 30× leverage, larger than Lehman or Morgan Stanley by capital base, 2.5× Fidelity Magellan.

Ch.9 — A Nobel Prize (1997, the strategic detour)

Every bank now had arbitrage desks. “We’d go to put on a trade, but when we started to nibble, the opportunity would vanish” (Rosenfeld).

Ch.10 — Bank of Volatility (Jan-Aug 1998 setup)

shorting equity volatility. Long-dated S&P 500 and European-index options at implied vol 19% when realized vol was ~15%.

Ch.11 — The Fall (August 1998: the 4B→2.28B collapse in one month)

Aug 17 — Russia debt moratorium and ruble devaluation. The “punctured moral hazard bubble” — first major crisis since 1990s where IMF/G7 did not bail out.

Ch.12 — The Human Factor (Sept 1-19, 1998)

Sept 2 — the leaked Meriwether letter. -44% August / -52% YTD disclosed to investors; one investor leaked to Bloomberg.

Ch.13 — At the Fed (Sept 20-28, 1998: the rescue)

Sept 20 (Sunday) — Fisher visits Greenwich. Peter Fisher (NY Fed deputy, 42), Dino Kos, and Treasury’s Gary Gensler arrive.

Ch.15 — Afterword 2010 — LTCM as Template, Not Exception

Ten years to the month after the LTCM rescue: Fannie/Freddie bailed out, Lehman fell, AIG rescued, Goldman/Morgan converted to commercial banks for FDIC access, Treasury bailed money market industry.
