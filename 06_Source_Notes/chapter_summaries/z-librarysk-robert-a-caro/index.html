The Power Broker: Robert Moses and the Fall of New York — Chapter Deep Dives

Ch.1 — Wait Until the Evening

Caro opens with two confrontations forty-five years apart. As a Yale undergraduate, Moses tries to deceive an alumnus donor, is told no by the swim captain, and resigns the team.

Ch.2 — Part I — The Idealist (Line of Succession, Yale, Home Away From Home)

Three chapters establish Moses’ background: German-Jewish merchant grandparents (Cohens and Silvermans), wealthy New Haven boyhood, mother Bella’s commanding personality and reform-philanthropy work, Yale undergraduate years (1905-09), and…

Ch.3 — Part II — The Reformer (Burning, Age of Optimism)

Moses returns from Oxford in 1913, marries Mary Sims, joins the Bureau of Municipal Research, and in 1914 enters reform Mayor John Purroy Mitchel’s administration.

Ch.4 — Part III opens — Curriculum Changes, Change in Major, The Taste of Power

Belle Moskowitz, Al Smith’s gatekeeper and political brain, brings Moses into Smith’s circle in 1919. Moses drafts the Reconstruction Commission report (1919) — a sweeping reorganization plan for New York State government, recommending con…

Ch.5 — A Dream; The Best Bill Drafter in Albany

Caro narrates Moses’ Long Island vision (40,000 acres of state parks, knit by parkways) and then the legislative weapon that made it possible: Chapter 122 of the Laws of 1924 establishing the Long Island State Park Commission and a State C…

Ch.6 — The Majesty of the Law; Creature of the Machine

Moses uses the 1924 act to bulldoze the Long Island barons (Taylor estate, Wheatley Hills, Northern State Parkway route fights).

Ch.7 — Driving; Changing; Curator of Cauliflowers; The Feather Duster

These chapters cover Moses’ Long Island and upstate park-building during 1925-1928, his unsuccessful 1934 run for Governor (the “Curator of Cauliflowers” sneer at his cabbage-growing brother-in-law’s farm), and his alliance/break with Smit…

Ch.8 — The Mother of Accommodation; New York City Before Robert Moses

Chapter 17 chronicles Moses’ political education in compromise — the long fights over the Northern State Parkway route around the rich North Shore estates.

Ch.9 — To Power in the City; One Year

In January 1934, La Guardia takes office and (after vicious negotiation) appoints Moses City Park Commissioner — but also lets him continue as State Parks chair, Long Island State Park Commission president, and then chair of the new Tribor…

Ch.10 — The Candidate; Order Number 129

Chapter 21 covers Moses’ 1934 gubernatorial run again from inside the campaign — what it revealed about his temperament.

Ch.11 — In the Saddle; Driving (Part 2)

By the late 1930s Moses holds twelve simultaneous offices (NYC Park Commissioner, Triborough chair, Long Island State Park Commission president, State Council of Parks chair, City Planning Commission member, etc.).

Ch.12 — Part V — Changing; Two Brothers; Changing; The Warp on the Loom

Caro’s longest single chapter, “Changing” (Ch. 25), is a 75-page interior portrait of how Moses’ personality shifted between the Yale idealist and the Bronx-Tremont evictor.

Ch.13 — And When the Last Law Was Down; Revenge; Monopoly; Quid Pro Quo

Chapters 29-32 cover the post-WWII consolidation. Title-VI federal highway money, Title I urban renewal money, and Triborough surplus together create an unprecedented construction war chest in Moses’ hands.

Ch.14 — Leading Out the Regiment; Moses and the Mayors; “RM”

These chapters cover Moses’ relationships with postwar mayors O’Dwyer (1946-50), Impellitteri (1950-53), and the early Wagner years (1954+).

Ch.15 — The Meat Ax; One Mile; One Mile (Afterward)

The most famous case study in The Power Broker. Moses’ Cross-Bronx Expressway plan from 1946 routes one mile through the densely-populated Jewish working-class neighborhood of East Tremont.

Ch.16 — The Highwayman; Point of No Return

These chapters cover Moses’ postwar highway program in the wider context of the 1956 Federal-Aid Highway Act (“Title VI” of which is the central federal cash flow).

Ch.17 — Rumors and the Report of Rumors; Tavern in the Town; Late Arrival

The Title I urban renewal scandals begin to break publicly in 1959. Mayor Wagner, under pressure from the City Club and reform Democrats, is forced to investigate.

Ch.18 — Mustache and the Bard; Off to the Fair; Nelson; The Great Fair

These chapters cover two parallel late-career projects: the 1964-65 New York World’s Fair (which Moses chaired) and Moses’ increasingly fraught relationship with Governor Nelson Rockefeller, who took office in 1959 and was the first NY gov…

Ch.19 — Old Lion, Young Mayor; The Last Stand

John Lindsay takes office as Mayor of New York City in January 1966 with a campaign promise to dismantle Moses’ transportation power by merging the Triborough Authority into a new city transportation agency.

Ch.20 — Old

Moses, at 79 in 1968, is consultant to MTA in name only. Ronan does not return his calls.
